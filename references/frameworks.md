## How mappings work

Fields. Every catalog control carries an `asi` field and a `mappings` object with three lists, `aiuc1`, `nist` and `aisvs`, whose entries hold a `ref` and a `relation`:

- `asi`: the ASI risks the control is designed to address. These identifiers place the control in the risk taxonomy; they do not claim that the control fully covers a risk.
- `aiuc1`: AIUC-1 control IDs (for example B006.1) rather than bare requirement IDs, because core and supplemental controls carry different weight.
- `nist`: AI RMF 1.0 subcategories (for example MANAGE 2.4) and NIST AI 600-1 suggested-action IDs (for example MG-2.4-002).
- `aisvs`: OWASP AISVS 1.0 requirement IDs in the version-prefixed form the AISVS project recommends (for example v1.0-C9.2.8). Items from AISVS Appendix C (AI-Assisted Secure Coding) are cited as v1.0-AC.x.y.

ISO/IEC 42001 has no field (see the ISO/IEC 42001 section).

Relations. Each `aiuc1`, `nist` and `aisvs` entry states how closely the control corresponds to the cited external item. Every relation reads from the control toward the external item, for every framework:

- **direct**: The control and the external item state essentially the same obligation.
- **partial**: The control and the external item overlap, and at least one of them includes obligations the other lacks. Where the external item is broader, as AI RMF subcategories and AI 600-1 actions usually are, the control achieves part of it; where the external item is narrower (for example limited to MCP, or to products that generate code), the control can achieve all of it and go further.
- **supports**: Implementing the control contributes to, or produces evidence toward, the external item without achieving it.

No mapping in catalog 0.1.0 is direct. AI RMF subcategories are never marked direct, because each one includes organization-level outcomes that no single control meets, and no AI 600-1 action is marked direct either. A record under this catalog never evidences a whole AI RMF subcategory or AI 600-1 action. A direct relation does not carry over the source's own conditions: AIUC-1 scoping, frequencies, third-party testing and auditor judgment still apply.

Other rules:

- Mappings are many to many. A control can cite several items, and one item can appear under several controls. A mapping never merges or restates a source requirement.
- An empty list means that no counterpart more specific than a generic anchor was found, not that the control matters less. Only T-04 has no AIUC-1 counterpart. AISVS has no counterpart for, among others, task-scope binding (A-07) and the system of record for consequential facts (T-08).
- [mappings.md](mappings.md) lists every control's mappings and relations. It is generated from the catalog by the repository's render script and is never edited by hand.
- In the source repository, `catalog/mappings.json` holds the justification for each mapping entry, its source URL and the source version checked. It is not part of the installed skill, so the installed bundle carries identifiers and relations only; read the justifications in the source repository before relying on a mapping for an assessment. Justifications paraphrase the sources and reproduce no AIUC-1 text.
- Control citations use the bare catalog ID (A-06). Records, claims and external mappings use the version-pinned form described in [profile-and-levels.md](profile-and-levels.md). Foreign identifiers sit in their own labeled columns, never in a control ID column.
- Catalog levels are computed from the capability profile, while AISVS levels are assurance depths an organization selects, so the scales are not interchangeable. Approval binding (A-11) is Level 1 here, while v1.0-C9.2.8, which partly corresponds to it, is AISVS Level 3 ([profile-and-levels.md](profile-and-levels.md)).

## AIUC-1

### Snapshot and structure

The mapping pins the AIUC-1 release of July 15, 2026, per the [official changelog](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/changelog/). On 2026-10-03 it was still the most recent release, and the changelog announced the next release for October 15, 2026.

How AIUC-1 is structured ([scoping](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/scoping), [January 15, 2026 changelog](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/changelog/2026-01-15)):

- AIUC-1 requirements are mandatory or optional, and a requirement becomes optional when the agent lacks the capability it addresses. Each requirement carries capability tags, for example Universal, Automation, Code-generation or External-facing.
- Each requirement lists core controls ("Should include") and supplemental controls ("May include"); auditors may accept alternative evidence that meets the requirement. Organizations opt into supplemental controls where they are relevant; supplemental controls are not needed to pass.
- Scoping ends in a Statement of Applicability signed off by the auditor. A mandatory requirement can be scoped out only with a documented, legitimate business reason that the auditor accepts.
- Frequency is an attribute of the requirement: every 3, 6 or 12 months. A 12-month frequency does not mean the controls behind it run once a year; access checks still run on every request.
- The certificate is valid for 12 months, and evaluations are needed at least every 3 months to keep it valid ([FAQ](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/faq)). B001 and D004 call for testing by expert third parties every 3 months, as do D002, C010 and C011 (hallucination, harmful-output and out-of-scope-output testing, outside this skill's scope) and C012 (customer-defined risks). Internal tests run with this skill do not satisfy these requirements. They produce a test corpus, reproducible results and remediation records that an assessor can use.

This skill's approval, containment, and logging recommendations may be stricter than a particular AIUC-1 control. They are not universal mandates attributed to AIUC-1.

### Where the catalog goes beyond AIUC-1 core

These controls go beyond AIUC-1 core controls; where the OWASP crosswalk names a related gap, it is cited:

- Cumulative budgets across a run and all its descendants (A-20; crosswalk Gaps 4 and 7). Core D003.2 requires rate limits and transaction caps, and its evidence examples include circuit breakers; budgets shared across descendants have no core home.
- Approval binding to the approver, the exact normalized action and arguments, target, policy and resource version, and expiry, consumed with the effect (A-11). AIUC-1's human approval for tool calls (D003.4) is supplemental and does not specify binding.
- Delegation bounded to the intersection of the delegating context's ceiling, the initiating principal's authority, the delegate's policy and the task's need, with re-authorization by the receiver (A-15; related to crosswalk Gap 2, which lists autonomy escalation and identity substitution, and Gap 1 on agent-to-agent authorization). Among AIUC-1 controls, only supplemental A003.3 bounds inherited permissions; core B008.1 authenticates agent-to-agent callers and supplemental E015.2 logs delegation chains, but neither bounds authority.

### Published crosswalks with the OWASP list

An official OWASP crosswalk, [AIUC-1: Crosswalks OWASP Top 10 For Agentic Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/aiuc-1-crosswalks-owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications/) (Version 1.0, May 2026), maps requirements in both directions and identifies eight areas where AIUC-1 may need new or expanded requirements, including agent identity, runtime containment and monitoring, supply chain attestation, cascading failure containment, and schema controls. These are gaps in AIUC-1, not in the OWASP list or in this catalog.

Both that crosswalk and the AIUC-1 [hosted mapping](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/crosswalks/owasp-agentic-top-10) (last updated May 27, 2026) predate the July 15, 2026 AIUC-1 release. The crosswalk also cites retired and pre-2026 identifiers and titles (for example E007, retired January 2026). Some of the gaps it describes have since narrowed: the April 15, 2026 release split agent identity and agent permission management into separate controls (now A003.2 and A003.3), added execution-level safeguards (B006.3) and agent execution logging (E015.2), and extended caller authentication to MCP and agent-to-agent interfaces (now B008.1). All of these except B008.1 are supplemental controls.

### Older mappings, retired identifiers and renumbering

- Retired: E007 was merged into E004, and E014 into E017, at the January 15, 2026 release, as shown in the [accountability requirements](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/accountability/). The catalog cites neither.

### Generative AI risk categories

AI 600-1 section 2 describes 12 risks that generative AI creates or makes worse. This framework's principle is: The model proposes. The application enforces. Section 2.9 describes the threat behind it: "Indirect prompt injection attacks occur when adversaries remotely (i.e., without a direct interface) exploit LLM-integrated applications by injecting prompts into data likely to be retrieved." (NIST AI 600-1, section 2.9, p. 11). Any retrieved content can carry instructions, so enforcement cannot depend on the model's judgment.

- Addressed by catalog controls, within this skill's scope:
  - 2.12 Value Chain and Component Integration: T-18, T-24, T-25.
  - 2.7 Human-AI Configuration, including automation bias in approvals and over-reliance on unverified agent output: A-12, A-13, T-26.
  - 2.4 Data Privacy, as disclosure boundaries and provider data routing: S-06, T-06, T-10.
- Partly addressed:
  - 2.9 Information Security. Indirect prompt injection: T-01, T-02, A-06, A-08, D-04. Data poisoning, which AI 600-1 defines as compromise of a training dataset: T-25 for fine-tuning and adapter data and, by analogy, T-07 for agent memory. The GAI-enabled offensive cyber capability that section 2.9 also describes, and the confidentiality of model weights, are outside this skill's scope.
  - 2.2 Confabulation, only where a confabulated value could drive a consequential action. Consequential values come from trusted sources and the system of record (S-05, A-06, T-08).

The same section-to-control relevance is recorded as a context note in `catalog/mappings.json`; the two lists name the same controls.
- Outside this skill's scope, and needing separate treatment: 2.1 CBRN Information or Capabilities; 2.3 Dangerous, Violent, or Hateful Content; 2.5 Environmental Impacts; 2.6 Harmful Bias and Homogenization; 2.8 Information Integrity; 2.10 Intellectual Property; 2.11 Obscene, Degrading, and/or Abusive Content.

### How this framework relates to other OWASP work

This framework applies the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 as its risk taxonomy inside a coding agent's build workflow: the catalog's controls name the ASI risks they are designed to address, and each control is enforced outside the model, tested adversarially through the real execution path and recorded as evidence, without claiming that any risk is fully covered. ### Builders and verifiers

[Praxen](https://open-agent-ai-security.github.io/praxen/) is an open-source tool, released by Exabeam under Apache-2.0 in June 2026, that installs as a Claude Code plugin. It compares an AI agent's declared policy with its code, deployment state and behavioral logs, reports where observed behavior drifts from intent, maps findings to the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026 and the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026, and scores the agent on the [RAISE framework](https://open-agent-ai-security.github.io/praxen/guide/RAISE.html) (Responsible AI Software Engineering), a six-category maturity model by Steve Wilson, scored from 0 to 5. Facts in this paragraph are from those pages, checked 2026-10-04.

STAND is unrelated to the RAISE framework; "RAISE" was this project's working name before the rename to STAND. The two serve different moments and are complementary. STAND works on the builder's side: it guides a coding agent while it builds or changes an agentic application, enforces controls outside the model, and produces per-control hostile tests and a record keyed by control ID. A verifier such as Praxen works after the fact on an agent that already exists, comparing its behavior with its declared policy and scoring its maturity. A STAND record, its tests and its threat model can serve as evidence for such a verifier, and a verifier's drift findings can feed STAND's change triage (N-09). Neither replaces the other, and no endorsement is implied in either direction.
